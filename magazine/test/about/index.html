About the cover from artist Juliet Bogan

As an Iranian-American Bahá’í artist, much of my painting practice attempts to imagine a homeland I do not know, while reaching for a future uniting people, land and spirit. This painting is simultaneously nostalgic and hopeful, evoking a community built into the landscape. Natural, vernal beauty merges with the beauty of service and stewardship of the earth, inspired by community-building activities we are undertaking in New York City. References range from my landscape photography in the Northeastern United States, to photographs sourced of Iran and Yemen where my grandparents grew up, and finally to the curving calligraphy of the Greatest Name, which I used as a template for the tumbling hills of the farmland.

About this issue

This issue explores the many ways creative expression can contribute to the transformation of individuals and communities, from music and visual art to the creative practices woven into grassroots efforts. The stories that follow offer glimpses of communities drawing on imagination, art and spiritual insights to contribute to the betterment of humanity.

The issue also includes the National Spiritual Assembly’s mid-year reflections, presented in English and Persian, alongside the magazine’s first publication in Simplified Chinese.
