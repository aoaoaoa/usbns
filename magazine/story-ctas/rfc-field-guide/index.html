Field guide

Capturing the story where you are

A short guide for anyone sending photographs or video. Most of it works with a phone.

  1. Be conscientious of your presence in the room
  2. Be ready for spontaneous moments
  3. Capture a series of angles
  4. Keep a steady hand
  5. Review your work on a bigger screen

Framing, from wide to close

Examples from this issue. The guide asks for a range: one wide, some mid, a few close.

Composition

Thirds
Balance the picture on a grid of thirds; place the subject on a line, not dead center.
Vanishing point
Lines that run into the distance add depth.
Depth and focus
A sharp subject against a softer background.
Over the shoulder
Catches warm exchanges between people.
Candid and posed
Both are welcome. Group photos let us see everyone’s face; no group is too small.

By situation

Audience
A wide shot with many faces clearly visible. Add reaction shots: one person listening, laughing, moved.
Stage
Keep the event’s title in the frame. Make sure the microphone doesn’t hide a speaker’s face. Look for warm engagement with the audience.
Community
Neighborhood gatherings can be spontaneous, intimate and delicate. A phone is fine: wipe the lens and set your focus. Use silent mode or a zoom so you don’t interrupt.
Action
Raise the shutter speed to match the light and the movement. Focus on one person; faces draw the eye.
Devotion
Devotion takes different forms and asks for a quiet, creative approach. Be especially mindful of your presence.

Before you send

Proposed starter specs, to be confirmed by the editors.

  • Send originalsFull-size files straight from the camera or phone, not screenshots or messaging copies.
  • Name the photographerEvery picture carries a credit.
  • Ask firstEveryone recognizable has agreed to be pictured. For children, a parent or guardian.
  • Say where and whenTown or neighborhood, state, and the date.
  • VideoSteady and horizontal for the photo library; vertical is welcome for social. Ten seconds per shot or longer.
